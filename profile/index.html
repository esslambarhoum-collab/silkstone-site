01 · Who we are

Silk is the garment. Stone is the factory.

03 · The way we work

Long-term, and written down.

We do not chase one-season orders. The factory is built for programmes that repeat: the same basics, in the same quality, delivered to plan year after year. Three commitments hold that together.

Photograph to follow
Sewing floor, wide
Partnership
Buyers deal with the owner directly, from the floor. Prices, capacity and problems are put on the table early. A programme is planned with the buyer's calendar, not against it.
Transparency
Every stage is managed in an integrated ERP: real-time monitoring of production, order-based tracking, in-line and final quality recorded digitally, and client access to production status and quality reports as they are produced.
Operational discipline
A full operation breakdown before the first piece is sewn, hourly output by operation, marker efficiency known on the order, and a zero defects policy enforced by three inspections rather than one at the end.
People
People are the asset the factory runs on. Continuous skill development, a safe, fair and inclusive workplace, and ethical, transparent management, audited under WRAP, SMETA and ISO 45001. Employee satisfaction is treated as a driver of stable production, not a cost.
Responsibility
Environmental, social and governance principles built into daily operation: responsible use of resources under ISO 14001, no hazardous chemicals in processing, and certified organic and recycled fibre programmes for buyers who need them.

04 · Capabilities

Fabric in. Garments out.

01

Fabric sourcing

Yarn and knitted fabric from Egyptian mills and partner mills abroad, through Aletex when the buyer prefers. Country of origin declared for all cotton. Better Cotton, organic and recycled yarn with transaction certificates.

02

Fabric inspection

Every incoming roll runs through the inspection machine. Defects, shade and weight are recorded against the roll number before it is released to the floor.

03

Computerised cutting

Digital markers, automatic cutting heads, every lay logged with plies and consumption. Each cut bundle carries the lay number so any garment traces back to its rolls.

04

Sewing, sixteen lines

Sixteen lines running, thirty-three at full capacity. Each style has a full operation breakdown before the first piece is sewn; hourly output by operation goes straight into the ERP.

05

Trims and labels

Neck labels, care labels, drawcords, eyelets, snaps, buttons and zips, sourced to the tech pack and pull-tested per batch.

06

Print and embroidery

Rotary and continuous printing, screen and transfer work, and an embroidery department, all beside the sewing floor.

07

Packing and dispatch

Final random inspection to the buyer's AQL, every garment through a calibrated metal detector, then folded, bagged, labelled and packed to the carton ratio. Cartons scanned at sealing and at loading.

06 · Quality and product safety

Checked three times. Written down every time.

Quality is a digitally managed system with a zero defects policy, audited to the standards our buyers ask for.

Before cutting

Fabric inspection

Every roll through the inspection machine. Defects, shade and weight recorded against the roll number and mill lot.

During sewing

In-line inspection

Checks inside each line while the garment is built, recorded by operation with the inspector's name and the corrective action.

After packing

Final inspection

Inspected to the buyer's AQL, then every piece through a calibrated metal detector in a secured zone before the carton is sealed.

  • Needles. Every broken needle logged, every fragment recovered and matched before a replacement is issued.
  • Metal. A calibrated metal detector checks every garment inside a secured zone before packing.
  • Pull tests. Buttons, snaps and drawcord tips tested per batch for durability and child safety.
  • Chemicals. No hazardous chemicals in stain removal or processing; restricted substance lists followed.
  • The ERP. In-line and final results recorded against order, line and operation. Buyers see quality reports as they are produced, and every carton traces back to its rolls.

10 · Location

Close to Europe. Built for export.

Egypt is the nearest large garment producer to Europe with duty-free access on three sides. It grows its own cotton, spins and knits it, and ships from the Mediterranean. The factory is about an hour from Cairo International Airport.

11 · People and responsibility

Run by the owner, on the floor.
